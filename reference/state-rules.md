# State rules that change the playbook

As of 2026-11-16. Not legal advice. Every row links to the source it came from; confirm with counsel before acting.

## Medicaid work requirements

- **Start:** January 1, 2027, in 44 states (41 expansion states, DC and partial-expansion states). Roughly 30% of Medicaid beneficiaries are expected to be subject. ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/11/medicaid-work-requirements-remain-on-track-despite-partial-vacatur))
- **Not subject:** Wyoming, Texas, Kansas, Mississippi, Alabama, Florida, South Carolina. ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/11/medicaid-work-requirements-remain-on-track-despite-partial-vacatur))
- **Requirement:** 80 hours a month of qualifying activity, verified by the state at least twice a year, using a review of the prior 12 months of claims and encounter data. Self-attestation of medical frailty is restricted.
- **Medical frailty categories (statutory):** blind or disabled; substance use disorders; disabling mental disorders; physical, intellectual or developmental disabilities that significantly impair activities of daily living; serious or complex medical conditions. On October 29, 2026 a federal court (*Massachusetts v. Oz*) vacated CMS's added requirement that people in these categories also show a functional impairment.
- **Hold-harmless option:** on November 13, 2026 CMS let states suspend disenrollments for non-compliance until June 30, 2027. States electing it still assess compliance from January 1.
- **Litigation:** Oregon, Minnesota, New Mexico and Washington filed a second lawsuit on November 5, 2026.
- **Early implementers:** Montana and Nebraska began before January 2027; Arkansas ran a soft launch from July 2026 without disenrollments before January. ([KFF tracker](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/medicaid-work-requirements-tracker-1115-waivers/))

**Why it matters for providers:** because states check the prior 12 months of claims and encounter data, complete and accurate diagnosis coding on your claims can be the evidence that keeps a medically frail patient covered. This is a documentation-accuracy point, never a reason to code anything the record does not support.

## Medicaid financing (hospitals)

- **Provider taxes:** frozen at enactment (July 4, 2025); in expansion states the safe harbor steps down from 6% toward 3.5% starting October 1, 2027 (FFY 2028). Nursing homes and ICFs are exempt from the phase-down. ([CLA](https://www.claconnect.com/en/resources/articles/26/obbba-for-providers))
- **State-directed payments:** capped at 100% of Medicare in expansion states and 110% in non-expansion states; amounts above the cap fall 10 percentage points a year from January 1, 2028. First-year cut estimated at $8.7 billion. ([HFMA](https://www.hfma.org/fast-finance/sdp-cuts-will-start-with-8-7-billion-in-2028-study/))
