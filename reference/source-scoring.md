# Source scoring

Every fact that changes an assumption, a date or a recommendation carries a credibility score and a depth score. policy-radar scores new sources with this rubric before anything reaches the leader.

## Credibility (1 to 5)

| Score | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | Official record or first-hand dataset | CMS fact sheets and rules, Federal Register, CBO, KFF analyses, HRSA, state Medicaid agency notices, Kaufman Hall Flash Report PDFs, Marsh and PwC primary surveys |
| 4 | Established firm or outlet working from primary material | AAMC, HFMA, law firm client alerts (Morgan Lewis, McDermott, BakerHostetler), Fierce Healthcare, Medical Economics, Healthcare Dive, rating agency coverage |
| 3 | Trade summary, or an interested party publishing data | Becker's, trade association surveys (AHA, AMA, MGMA), advocacy groups with primary tracking (PESP) |
| 2 | Summary of a summary, or marketing content | Vendor blogs, aggregator posts, consulting marketing pieces |
| 1 | Unsourced or anonymous | Social posts, forums |

## Depth (1 to 5)

5 shows full data and method; 4 shows the numbers and where they came from; 3 gives the key figures; 2 gives a headline figure; 1 is a headline with nothing behind it.

## Rules

1. An assumption changes only on a source scored 4 or 5, or on two independent sources scored 3.
2. Label advocacy sources in the text ("the AMA, an advocacy group, calculates...").
3. Keep "proposed rule" and "final rule" distinct every time.
4. When a secondary source and the primary disagree, the primary wins, and the disagreement is logged. Example from the outline this plugin came from: a trade summary said eight states passed PE laws in 2026; the primary report counts nine laws in six states plus a Rhode Island rule.
5. Every number in a leader-facing output names its source inside the sentence.
